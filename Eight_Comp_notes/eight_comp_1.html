Emerging technologies are new and growing ideas in the world of computers. They change how we live, talk, travel, and learn. They make our daily tasks much easier and faster.

IoT (Internet of Things): This is a giant network where everyday objects are connected to the internet.

  • How it works: Objects have tiny sensors and software so they can "talk" to each other without humans helping.
  • Example: A "smart" fridge that tells your phone when you are out of milk, or an AC you can turn on from your car.

Embedded Systems: This is a small computer hidden inside a machine to do one specific job.

  • How it works: It uses a tiny brain (microprocessor) to control how a machine runs.
  • Example: The computer inside a washing machine that controls the timer, or the system in a car that controls the brakes.

Edge Computing: This is a way to process data very close to where it is created, instead of sending it far away to a main server.

  • How it works: It makes things faster by handling information "on the spot".
  • Example: Traffic cameras in a city that process data right at the street corner to change the lights instantly.

Data Analytics: This is the process of studying large amounts of information to find patterns or "trends".

  • How it works: It helps companies look at what happened in the past to make better decisions for the future.
  • Example: A hospital looking at patient records to find the best way to treat a flu.

5G stands for the 5th Generation of mobile networks. It is the newest way for our phones and computers to connect to the internet.

The Main Benefits of 5G: There are three big reasons why 5G is great:

  • Faster Phone Service: Your mobile phone will work much better and won't lag or slow down.
  • Quick Downloads: You can download big files, like movies or games, in just a few seconds.
  • Better Connections: 5G allows a huge number of devices (like phones, smartwatches, and computers) to connect to each other at the same time without any problems.

Example: Downloading a full movie in just a few seconds on your phone.

3D Holographic Imaging is a modern technology that uses special light beams called lasers to create pictures. These pictures are called holograms. This technology uses lasers to create pictures that look like real, 3D objects.

Example: Seeing a 3D "ghost" or a floating heart in a science museum.

Artificial Intelligence (AI): AI is when computers are taught to "think" like humans to solve hard problems.

  • How it works: It uses smart programs to do tasks that usually need a human brain.
  • Daily Life Example: A computer playing a very difficult game of chess.

Machine Learning: Machine Learning is a special part of Artificial Intelligence (AI). It is a way to make computers "smart" without a human telling them every single step.

  • How it works: The computer looks at huge amounts of data and learns from "experience" without a human teaching it every step.
  • Example: Google Maps learning the fastest way home by looking at traffic from previous days.

Robotics is the science of building and using robots. Robots are smart machines made to do jobs that people usually do. Robots are used to do tasks that are too boring, too difficult, or too dangerous for humans.

Use of Robots: You can find robots working in many places today:

  • Making Cars: Robots are very strong and can put car parts together quickly.
  • Packaging: In factories, robots put items into boxes and get them ready to be sent to shops.
  • Science Labs: Robots help scientists by doing very careful tests and research.
  • Cleaning Homes: Some small robots can move around your floor and vacuum up dust by themselves.

Computer-Assisted Translation (CAT) is when we use smart computer programs to change text from one language to another (like changing English into Urdu or Spanish).

Why we use CAT:

  • Speed: Computers can translate thousands of pages very quickly.
  • Accuracy: It helps businesses share information correctly so everyone understands the same thing.

Example: Imagine you buy a box of noodles with instructions written in Chinese. Without CAT, you have to find a person who speaks Chinese to read it for you, which is slow and difficult. With CAT, you point your phone camera at the box, and an app instantly changes the Chinese words into your language.

Virtual Reality is a technology that uses computers to create a fake world that looks and feels real.

  • How it works: It creates a whole new world around you. You are not just looking at a screen; you feel like you are inside the world. To see this world, you usually have to wear a special helmet or goggles over your eyes.
  • Example: Video Games: You can play a game where you feel like you are walking inside a giant castle or exploring a different planet.

A Distributed Application is a special kind of computer program that does not just live on one computer. Instead, it is spread out across many different computers (called servers) that are all connected in a network.

  • How it works: It runs on many computers at the exact same time to finish one single job.
  • Why we use it: Sometimes a job is too big or there is too much work for just one computer to handle. By using many computers, the work becomes easy.
  • Examples: Apps like Facebook or Instagram use thousands of computers to show photos to millions of people.

Blockchain is a very secure way to store digital information. It is like a digital notebook where once you write something, it can never be rubbed out or changed.

  • How it works: Information is collected into small groups called "blocks". When a block is full, it is "locked" and connected to the block before it, creating a long chain. Once a block is part of the chain, the information inside cannot be changed or deleted.

Purpose of Blockchain Technology:

  • Secure Data: Keeps data safe and hard to change.
  • No Middleman Needed: People can exchange data or money directly.
  • Track Records: Helps track transactions, ownership, or any history.
  • Prevent Fraud: Makes it difficult for anyone to cheat or hack the system.

Example: Banks using it to keep a perfect record of money being moved.

Data Communication is a fancy name for "sending information". It is the process of moving information from one device to another through a network. Think of it like two people talking on the phone; they are communicating data (voice) through a system.

Components of Data Communication: To send a message, you need four main things. If one is missing, the communication will not work!

  • Sender: is a person/entity that start the communication.
  • Message: is the information that the sender wishes to share.
  • Medium: is the mean or path by which the message is sent (like a wire or Wi-Fi).
  • Receiver: is the person/entity who receives the message.

Client-Server Architecture is a network model in which computers are divided into two main roles:

1. 🖥️Client: A client is a device or program that requests services or data.

  • Examples: web browsers, mobile apps, or a computer requesting a webpage.

2. 🌐Server: A server is a powerful computer or system that provides services, data, or resources to clients.

  • Examples: web servers, database servers, file servers.

How it Works (Example: Online Banking)

Imagine you want to check your bank account on your phone:

  • The Client (You): You use your phone or computer to ask for your balance.
  • The Request: You send your username and password to the bank.
  • The Server (The Bank): The bank's computer checks your name and password.
  • The Service: The server sends your balance back to your screen.

Computer Network: What are the benefits of using networks (SRQ 2)

A Computer Network is when two or more computers or devices (like printers or phones) are joined together.

benefits of using networks: We connect them so they can share things, such as:

  • Hardware: Sharing one printer between many computers.
  • Software: Sharing programs or files with each other.

Types of Computer Networks

There are different types of networks. We name them based on how much area they cover.

Personal Area Network (PAN)

  1. This is the smallest type of network.
  2. It is a network for one person.
  3. It covers a very small area (less than 10 meters).
  4. It connects personal devices like a phone and headphones.

Example: Your phone and your wireless earbuds.

Local Area Network (LAN)

  1. A LAN connects devices in a small area.
  2. It is a network for a small group of people.
  3. It covers a small area like a school or a home (one building).
  4. It connects devices like computers and printers in one building.

Example: Your school computer lab

Metropolitan Area Network (MAN)

  1. A MAN is a medium-sized network.
  2. It is a network for a whole city.
  3. It covers a medium area like a town or a large campus.
  4. It connects many smaller networks (LANs) across a city.

Example: It covers a whole city or a large university campus with many buildings

Wide Area Network (WAN)

  1. A WAN is a huge network that covers the whole world.
  2. It is a network for the whole world.
  3. It covers a large area like different cities and countries.
  4. It connects many smaller networks together globally.

Example: A bank with branches in many cities.

Virtual Private Network (VPN)

  1. It is a network for private and safe use.
  2. It covers the internet by creating a secure path.
  3. It connects a user to a bank or a website securely.

Example: When you use an ATM (cash machine), a VPN helps keep your bank account information safe and private while it talks to the bank.

A communication device is a tool that helps computers talk to each other.

Network Hub

  • It is a simple device for a small network (LAN).
  • It connects many computers together.
  • It sends information to everyone in the network, but only the right computer keeps it.

Network Switch: What is the role of network switch (SRQ 3)

  • It is a smart device for a wired network.
  • It connects computers and remembers their "address" (MAC table).
  • It sends information directly to the right computer instead of everyone.

Router

  • It is a device that acts like a traffic police officer.
  • It connects two or more different networks (like joining your home LAN to the Internet).
  • It finds the best and fastest path for your message to travel.

Network Interface Card (NIC)

  • It is a small card or chip inside your computer.
  • It allows your computer to join a network.
  • It connects your device to the internet using a wire or a wireless signal.

Wireless Access Point (WAP)

  • It is a device that adds Wi-Fi to a wired network.
  • It allows more people to connect wirelessly.
  • It extends the area of the network so you can use it further away.

Light Fidelity (LiFi)

  • It is a new technology that uses light to send data.
  • It provides very fast and safe internet using light bulbs.
  • It covers a large area if you use many lights in a building.

Smart Phones

  • It is a powerful mobile device with a touch screen.
  • It connects to the internet, plays music, and takes photos.
  • It acts like a small computer that fits in your pocket.

📡 Transmission Media

👉 Transmission media are paths that carry data from one place to another.

👉 Data can travel in two ways:

1. 🔌 Guided Media (Wired Network)

A wired network uses cables like Ethernet, coaxial, or fiber-optic to connect devices. Devices are usually fixed in one place. Wired networks are fast, stable, and more secure. However, installation can be difficult and expensive because of the cables.

Example: Coaxial Cable, Twisted Pair Cable, Fiber-Optic Cable

2. 📶 Unguided Media (Wireless Network):

A wireless network uses airwaves like WiFi, Bluetooth, or satellites to connect devices. Devices can move freely while connected. Wireless networks are easy to install and expand, but their speed can be slightly slower, and security is less strong.

Example: Radio waves, Microwave, Infrared

1. 🧵Coaxial Cable

  • It is cable that sends data and protects it from interference.
  • Made of copper wire with a special metal cover.
  • The metal shield protects signals from disturbance.
  • Mostly used by cable TV companies.
  • Connects satellite dish to homes and offices.

📌 Example: Cable TV connection

2. 🔗 Twisted Pair Cable

  • It is a cable that sends data safely with two twisted wires.
  • Made of two insulated wires twisted together.
  • Twisting helps to reduce signal interference.
  • Used in telephone lines and internet networks.
  • Common in data and voice communication.

📌 Example: Internet (LAN/Ethernet cable)

3. 💡Fiber-Optic Cable

  • It is a cable that sends data using light signals.
  • Made of very thin glass fibers.
  • Uses light pulses to send data.
  • Provides very high speed internet.
  • Can send data over long distances.
  • Has high bandwidth (more data at once).

📌 Example: Fast internet connections

1. 📻Radio Waves

  • 👉 Waves that carry data wirelessly over long distance.
  • Used in many devices like 📱 Mobile phones, 📻 Radio, 📺 Television.
  • Can travel through walls and buildings.
  • Covers large areas.

📌 Example: FM radio, mobile signals

2. 📡Microwaves

  • 👉 Waves that carry data in a straight line (line-of-sight).
  • Travel in a straight path.
  • Need no obstacles between sender and receiver.
  • Use high frequency signals.
  • Can send voice, video, and data at high speed.

📌 Example: Satellite communication

3. Infrared (IR) Waves

  • 👉 Waves that carry data using light for short distance.
  • Very cheap and easy to use.
  • Works over short distance only.
  • Cannot pass through walls.
  • Used in simple devices.

📌 Examples: TV remote, Car remote lock

Wireless communication:

Wireless communication is a way to send information (data) from one device to another without using any wires or cables.

Benefits:

  • Easy to setup: It is easy to install and fix.
  • Move around: You can talk or use the internet while moving from one place to another.
  • Fast: It sends information very quickly.
  • Growing is easy: It is simple to add more users or devices to the network.

1. Cellular Communication

This is the system used for mobile phones. It divides the land into small areas called "cells." It helps us talk (voice) and use the internet (data) with high speed.

  • Base Station: A main tower that connects your phone to the network.
  • Relay Station: A station that catches a signal and sends it further to help it reach longer distances.
  • Mobile: A device (like your phone) that you carry to use the network from anywhere.

2. Satellite Technology

A Communication Satellite is a machine in space. It helps people in different parts of the world talk to each other. It covers very large areas.

  • Satellite: A machine sent into space that circles the Earth. It receives signals, makes them stronger, and sends them back down.
  • Ground Station: Equipment on Earth used to send signals up to the satellite or receive them.
  • Uplink: When a signal travels from the Earth up to the satellite.
  • Downlink: When a signal travels from the satellite down to the Earth.

3. Global Positioning System (GPS)

GPS is a system that uses satellites to help people find exactly where they are on Earth (navigation). It is also used to find the distance between two objects.

4. Bluetooth

Bluetooth is a way to connect devices without wires over a short distance. It is used to connect:

  • Mobile phones and computers.
  • Headphones and speakers.
  • Printers and other gadgets.
