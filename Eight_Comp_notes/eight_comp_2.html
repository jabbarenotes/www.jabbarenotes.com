A spreadsheet is a computer application software used to store and organize data in a tabular form.

Microsoft Excel: Microsoft Excel is a computer program used to create spreadsheets.

Microsoft Excel is used for many purposes:

  • It is used to store data like names, marks, and records
  • It is used to organize data in rows and columns
  • It is used to perform calculations (addition, subtraction, etc.)
  • It is used to prepare result sheets and reports
  • It is used to create charts and graphs
  • It is used to manage accounts and expenses
  • It is used to save time and reduce mistakes

Example: Teachers use Excel to make students’ result sheets easily.

Worksheet:

  • A worksheet is a single page in a spreadsheet.
  • It is made of rows and columns.
  • It is used to enter and organize data.
  • Example: Worksheet = one page 📄

Workbook:

  • A workbook is a file that contains one or more worksheets.
  • It is like a notebook with many pages.
  • Example: Workbook = whole notebook 📘
  • A cell is the cross-point where a column and row meet.
  • A cell address is a combination of the column (shown by letters A, B, C, etc.) and row (recognized by numbers 1, 2, 3, etc.), respectively.
  • From Programs, click on Microsoft Office > Microsoft Excel.
  • You have the option of selecting a blank document, opening a recent document, or choosing from a template.
  • Blank document is selected by default.
  1. Open Excel: Click Start > Microsoft Office > Microsoft Excel.
  2. Select File Type:
    • Blank Workbook: A new, empty page (selected by default).
    • Recent: Opens your old work.
    • Template: A pre-made design (like a timetable).
  3. Click to Start: Choose Blank Workbook to begin typing on a clean sheet.

1. Home Tab (The Design Tool)

This is for changing how your text and boxes look.

  • Cut & Copy: "Pick up" or "make a twin" of your information.
  • Paste: "Drop" the information into a new box.
  • Format Painter: Copies the style (like color or size) from one box to another.
  • Font: Changes the handwriting style, size, or makes it bold.
  • Alignment: Moves text to the left, right, or center of the box.
  • Wrap Text: If a sentence is too long, it pushes it to a new line inside the same box.
  • Merge & Center: Joins many small boxes to make one large box.

2. Insert Tab (The Adding Tool)

Use this to put new things onto your page.

  • Insert Pictures: Adds photos from your computer.
  • Insert Charts: Turns numbers into a picture, like a Bar Graph.
  • Header & Footer: Adds a title at the top or a page number at the bottom of every page.

3. Page Layout Tab (The Printing Tool)

This gets your page ready to be printed on paper.

  • Themes: Changes all colors and styles at once.
  • Margins: Changes the empty white space around the edges of the paper.
  • Orientation: Choose Portrait (standing tall) or Landscape (lying wide).
  • Size: Picks the paper size (like A4).

4. Formulas Tab (The Math Tool)

This tab does the counting for you.

  • Insert Function: Helps you use math rules to find answers.
  • AutoSum: A fast button to add up a list of numbers instantly.

5. Data Tab (The Organizing Tool)

Use this when you have a lot of information.

  • Sort: Puts things in order (like A to Z or 1 to 10).
  • Filter: Hides what you don't need so you can see only specific items.

6. Review & View Tabs (The Checking Tools)

  • Spelling: Checks for writing mistakes.
  • Thesaurus: Suggests different words with the same meaning.
  • Normal View: The regular way to look at your work.
  • Zoom: Makes the screen look bigger or smaller.

An Excel Formula is a math instruction. It tells the computer to do math using numbers in different boxes (cells).

The Golden Rule: Every formula must start with an equal sign (=).

Example: Adding Marks (Sum)

Imagine you want to add the marks of 4 subjects.

  • The Formula: = C4 + D4 + E4 + F4
  • What it does: It adds the numbers in boxes C4, D4, E4, and F4 together.

Example 2: Finding Percentage

This helps you see how well you did out of 100.

  • The Formula: = G4 / 80 * 100

The Formula Bar allows you to view, enter, and edit the data or formulas contained in the active cell.

A Function is a "ready-made" formula. Instead of typing a long math problem, you use a special word to tell Excel what to do. It is like a shortcut that does the work for you.

1. SUM( ) Function: This is used to add numbers together quickly.

  • Example: =SUM(C4:F4)
  • It adds all the numbers in the boxes from C4 to F4.

2. AVERAGE( ) Function: This finds the middle value or the average of your marks.

  • Example: =AVERAGE(C4:F4)
  • It adds the marks and divides them by how many subjects there are.

3. IF( ) Function: This is the "Decision" tool. It checks a rule and gives one of two answers.

  • Example: =IF(I4 >= 60, "Pass", "Fail")
  • If the mark in box I4 is 60 or more, it writes Pass. If it is less than 60, it writes Fail.

4. MAX( ) Function: This finds the highest (biggest) number in a list.

  • Example: =MAX(G4:G8)
  • It looks at all the marks and picks the best one, like finding out who is the tallest student in your class.

5. MIN( ) Function: This finds the lowest (smallest) number in a list.

  • Example: =MIN(G4:G8)
  • It looks at all the marks and picks the lowest one, like finding the cheapest item in a shop.

Sorting simply means putting your information in a specific order. It helps you find what you are looking for much faster.

Types of Sorting: There are two main ways to arrange your data:

  • 1. Ascending Order (Small to Big): This is like counting 1, 2, 3... or putting names from A to Z.
  • 2. Descending Order (Big to Small): This is like counting backwards 10, 9, 8... or putting names from Z to A.

Example: The Result Sheet

Imagine you have a list of students and their marks. To see who did the best, you would sort the Percentage column in Descending Order.

  • The student with 99% will be at the very top.
  • The student with the lowest mark will be at the bottom.

A Chart is a way to show your numbers as a picture. Instead of looking at a long list of numbers, you can see them as shapes and colors. This makes the information much easier to understand.

Types of Charts: Excel has many styles of charts. Some common ones are:

  • Column Chart: Uses tall bars (standing up).
  • Bar Chart: Uses long bars (lying down).
  • Line Chart: Uses a line to show if numbers go up or down.
  • Pie Chart: A circle cut into "slices," like a real pizza.
[Image showing different types of Excel charts: Column, Bar, Line, and Pie charts]
  1. Select the data: Highlight the boxes (cells) that have the numbers and names you want to show.
  2. Click Insert: Go to the Insert Tab at the top and find the Charts section.
  3. Pick your style: Click on the type of chart you want (like a Column or Pie chart).

Every chart has different parts to help you read it:

  • Chart Title: The name of the chart (at the top).
  • Chart Area: The whole box where the chart is drawn.
  • Vertical Axis: The line going up and down (usually shows the numbers).
  • Horizontal Axis: The line going left to right (usually shows the names).
