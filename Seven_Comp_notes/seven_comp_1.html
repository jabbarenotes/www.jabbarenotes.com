Emerging technologies are new ideas and tools that are being made right now. They are expected to be ready for everyone to use within five to ten years.

These technologies will have a big impact on our:

  • Daily life
  • Business
  • Communication
  • Travel

Artificial Intelligence (AI) is the ability of computer systems to think and act like humans. These systems can do tasks such as solving problems, understanding languages, playing games, and making decisions.

Uses of Artificial Intelligence (AI):

  • Making transport safer (like self-driving cars).
  • Understanding and recognizing speech.
  • Playing games and controlling robots.

Biometrics is when parts of a person's body are used for identification. It means using your own body to show who you are. Your body is your password!

Examples of Biometric parts:

  • Finger prints
  • Eye recognition
  • Face recognition
  • Voice recognition

We use biometric data to open and use Smartphones, ATMs, Laptops.

Vision enhancement is used to help people see objects better. It makes things look larger or clearer.

It helps us see by using:

  • It helps people who have weak eyesight to see more clearly.
  • It supports people who are visually impaired (cannot see well).
  • It makes reading and watching easier using special devices.
  • It helps in daily life activities like reading signs, books, or screens.

Robotics is technology that makes machines called robots. These machines do tasks that humans used to do.

Robots are used for:

  • Diffusing landmines and bombs.
  • Fighting fires to keep people safe.
  • Exploring space where humans cannot go.
  • Lifting very heavy objects easily.
  • Performing surgery and serving as waiters in restaurants.
  • A robot in a factory can assemble a car faster and more accurately than humans.

Computer Assisted Translation is a way to translate languages using computer software. It helps and makes the translation process easier.

Examples of tools used:

  • Spell checkers
  • Grammar checkers
  • Electronic dictionaries

How it helps us:

  • Helps travelers talk and understand others easily when they visit different countries.
  • It helps people from different countries work together and build strong relationships.

3D and holographic imaging is a way to show products, objects, and moving pictures in three dimensions. This makes the images look real, like they are standing in front of you.

How it is changing the world:

  • The military uses 3D holographic maps for "battle-spaces."
  • It helps soldiers practice and train for their missions more effectively.

Virtual Reality (VR) is a computer-made world. It shows a three-dimensional image or environment that looks real.

A person can talk to and move things in this world in a way that feels physical. To do this, they use special electronic equipment:

  • Headset worn on the head to see the virtual world.
  • Controllers worn on the hands to touch or move objects.

Augmented Reality makes our experience of the world better. It does not create a fake world. Instead, it adds digital information like images or text on top of the real world we see.

How it works:

  • It takes a natural environment (what you see with your eyes).
  • It puts digital layers (pictures or words) over it.

Example of use: In education, AR can show 3D models of the human body or planets.

Self-Driving Cars are smart vehicles that can drive themselves. They use special technology to move safely without a human driver.

How the car works:

  • The sensors collect information from the surroundings.
  • The computer system analyzes the data.
  • The AI makes decisions.
  • The car moves safely based on those decisions.

3D Printing uses computer software to make a real, solid object. It can build things of different shapes, sizes, and colors.

How it works:

  • First, a picture (design) of the object is made on a computer.
  • The computer breaks the design into many thin layers.
  • The 3D printer builds the object layer by layer using material like plastic.
  • Slowly, the full object is created.

Cloud Storage (CS) is a way to save data on computers that are far away. These computers are called remote servers. You can get to your data through the Internet or the "cloud."

How it works:

  • When you save a file, it is sent through the internet.
  • The data is stored on powerful computers called servers.
  • These servers are kept in special places called data centers.
  • You can open your files anytime using the internet.

Open-source software is a type of computer program that is shared with its source code. This means the original instructions used to build the software are open for everyone to see.

Key features:

  • People can download and use it for free
  • They can study how the software works
  • They can change or improve the code
  • They can share it with others

Examples of Open-source software: Open Office, LINUX, Mozilla Firefox

The system unit is the case that holds all the electronic parts of a computer. Inside this case, you will find the motherboard, power supply, CPU, RAM, ROM, and hard disk.

i. Motherboard:
The motherboard is the main circuit board. It acts as a central hub that connects all the parts of the computer together.

ii. Central Processing Unit (CPU):
The CPU is the "brain" of the computer. It processes and carries out instructions. Its speed is measured in Hertz (Hz). It has two main parts:

  • Control Unit (CU): This part acts like a traffic policeman. It controls the flow of information and coordinates all other units.
  • Arithmetic Logic Unit (ALU): This part does all the math and logic (comparing numbers).

iii. Memory Unit:
The main memory (Primary Memory) is where the CPU stores and finds information quickly.

  • RAM (Random Access Memory): This is temporary memory. It holds programs that are currently running. If you turn off the power, all data in RAM is lost.
  • ROM (Read Only Memory): This is permanent memory. It stays even when the power is off.

Computer memory is measured in units. These units tell us how much information a computer can store. The most common units are Byte, Kilobyte, Megabyte, Gigabyte, and Terabyte.

Measurements:

  • 1 Bit: This is a Binary Digit (the smallest unit, either 0 or 1).
  • 8 Bits = 1 Byte: A Byte can hold one character, like a letter or a number.
  • 1024 Bytes = 1 KB (Kilobyte)
  • 1024 KB = 1 MB (Megabyte)
  • 1024 MB = 1 GB (Gigabyte)
  • 1024 GB = 1 TB (Terabyte)
  • 1024 TB = 1 PB (Petabyte)
  • 1024 PB = 1 EB (Exabyte)

Easy Tip: Each new unit is 1024 times bigger than the one before it!

RAM ROM
RAM stores operating system, application programs and data in use. ROM contains programs used for booting up the computer and BIOS.
Data in RAM can be modified. Data in ROM cannot be modified.
RAM is volatile memory. ROM is non-volatile memory.
Types of RAM: Dynamic RAM, Static RAM Types of ROM: PROM, EPROM, EEPROM, UVEPROM

Communication ports are the connection points that allow the computer to talk to other devices.

External Ports (Outside the Case):

  • HDMI: Transmits high-quality audio and video between devices like a computer and a TV.
  • USB: Connects common devices like a mouse, keyboard, and printer.
  • USB Type-C: A newer, smaller port used for charging and data.
  • Ethernet: Used to connect a network cable for internet or network access.
  • VGA: Used to send video signals to monitors and projectors.
  • S-Video Port: Sends video signals by splitting color and brightness.

Internal Ports & Slots (On the Motherboard):

  • SATA: Used to connect the hard drive to the motherboard.
  • PCI-express: Slots used for expansion cards like Graphics Card or Sound Card.

Peripherals are all the devices connected to a computer that are not part of the main system unit. They are divided into two groups: Input Devices and Output Devices.

1. Input Devices: These devices allow you to enter data into the computer.
Examples: Keyboard, Mouse, Microphone, Scanner, Webcam

2. Output Devices: These devices take information from the computer and present it to you.
Examples: Display Screen, Printer, Speaker, Headset, Multimedia Projector

The keyboard is the most commonly used input device for entering data into the computer. It is a main tool that allows people to communicate with the machine.

Key Features:

  • It consists of many different types of keys.
  • It allows computer users to enter:
    • Letters (A–Z)
    • Numbers (0–9)
    • Special Symbols (such as @, #, $, &, etc.)

The mouse is a small, hand-held pointing device used to interact with a computer. It is a key tool for navigating the screen.

Key Features:

  • It has two buttons and a scroll wheel at the top.
  • It controls the movement of the cursor (pointer) on the screen.
  • It is used to select folders, icons, files, and different options in programs.
  • It is used for tasks like drawing graphics and executing (running) computer programs.

The microphone is an input device that allows computer users to put sound (audio) into a computer, tablet, or phone.

Uses:

  • It is very commonly used on apps like WhatsApp.
  • It is used for chatting and web conferencing (online meetings) over the internet.

The scanner is an input device that allows computer users to take physical items and bring them into the digital world.

Uses:

  • It allows users to scan images, printed text, or even handwriting.
  • It converts these physical items into digital images.
  • The scanned material can be displayed on the computer screen.
  • Once digital, the material can be edited or changed.
  • It can be used as part of a document on the computer.

A webcam is a camera connected to a computer that is used to take pictures and record videos. It can also send live video over the internet.

Key Features:

  • It is used for online meetings and video chats (like Zoom or Google Meet).
  • We can capture still images.
  • It is used to show live video on the internet.
  • It is used to stream live video in real time.

The display screen is the most commonly used output device. It allows the user to see what the computer is doing in real time.

Key Features:

  • It provides a softcopy (a visual version) of the user's interaction with the computer.
  • It is used to display different types of information generated by the computer, including: Images, Text, Video, Graphics.

The printer is an output device that takes digital information from the computer and turns it into a physical form.

Key Features:

  • It accepts text, images, and graphics from the computer.
  • It prints this information onto paper.

Speakers:

  • Speakers are devices that play sound loudly.
  • They send sound to the whole room.
  • Used for music, videos, and games.

Example: Speakers are used when watching a movie with friends.

Headsets:

  • A headset is a combination of headphones and a microphone.
  • It is worn on the head.
  • It gives private sound directly to the ears.
  • The microphone helps to speak during calls or online classes.

Example: Headsets are used in online classes or gaming for clear and private sound.

A multimedia projector is an output device used to display computer-generated content on a large scale. It is ideal for sharing information with a large group of people.

Key Features:

  • It is used to display computer content to many people at the same time.
  • It helps in teaching and learning in classrooms.
  • It is used for business presentations and meetings.
  • It can show videos, slides, and pictures on a big screen.

Softcopy: Information you see on a screen (temporary).

Hardcopy: Information printed on paper (permanent).

A computer network is made when two or more computers are connected together. The main purpose of this connection is to communicate and share information.

Components of a Computer Network:

A computer network works with five main parts:

Server:
A powerful computer that stores data and programs. It shares resources like files, printers, and internet with other computers.

Client:
Any normal computer (like a student’s PC) that uses the data and services provided by the server.

Transmission Media:
These are the paths used to send data between computers. They can be wires (cables) or wireless signals (Wi-Fi).

Connecting Devices:
These are devices that help computers connect to the network, such as: Network Interface Card (NIC), Switch, Router, Wireless Access Point (WAP).

Network Operating System (NOS):
This is special software installed on the server. It helps control the network and allows users to easily access files, apps, and resources.
