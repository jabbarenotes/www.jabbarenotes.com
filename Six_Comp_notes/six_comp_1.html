ICT stands for Information and Communication Technology. It is a group of digital tools and machines that help us create, save, and share information with others.

We use ICT in our daily life, and it is almost everywhere. We can see it in schools, homes, hospitals, and workplaces.

ICT is used in many fields such as:

  • Business
  • Education
  • Entertainment
  • Health
  • Transport

A computer is a fast electronic machine. It takes in data, works on it quickly, and stores it for us without making mistakes.

We use many small computers every day before we even touch a laptop. These tiny computers are called microprocessors.

You can find these tiny computers in many things:

  • In the kitchen: They are in your alarm clock, toaster, and microwave.
  • At home: They are in your electric toothbrush and TV remote.
  • On the road: They are in car music players and traffic lights.

The history of computers is divided into five generations. Each generation shows how computers improved over time.

1. First Generation

These computers used vacuum tubes. They were very big and filled a whole room. They were slow, expensive, and used a lot of electricity.

2. Second Generation

These computers used transistors instead of vacuum tubes. They became smaller, faster, and cheaper. They were also more reliable. One transistor replaced about 40 vacuum tubes.

3. Third Generation

These computers used Integrated Circuits (ICs). They became even smaller and faster. They also had better memory and cost less.

4. Fourth Generation

These computers used microprocessors. They became very small, very fast, and had large storage. They are the computers we mostly use today. A microprocessor is a single chip that does all the work of a computer.

5. Fifth Generation

These computers use Artificial Intelligence (AI). They can understand human language and make decisions. Robots are examples of this generation.

Here is how we use it in different parts of our lives:

ICT in Communication:

  • Print Media: This is information on paper, like newspapers, magazines, and books.
  • Digital Media: This is information on electronic devices. It lets us send messages instantly using smartphones, social media, and emails.

ICT in Measurement and Weather:

  • Digital Experiments: Scientists use computer programs called simulations to see how things might happen in the future.
  • Weather Forecasting: ICT devices collect data about temperature, wind, and rain from land and satellites to tell us the weather forecast.

ICT in Industries and Science: (SRQ 3)

  • Robots: These are smart machines used to build cars, pack boxes, or even help doctors in surgery.
  • Exploration: High-tech robots like the Mars Rover explore other planets, while others help farmers harvest crops.

ICT in Business:

  • Computers are a must-have for every office. They are used to make bills, reports, and presentations.
  • You can find them in banks, hotels, and shops to help work run smoothly.

A robot is a man-made machine that can work like a human. It can do tasks automatically or be controlled from a distance. The study of robots is called robotics. Here are some interesting facts:

  1. The word “robot” comes from a Czech word “robota”, which means forced work or labor.
  2. The first humanoid robot was Elektro, built in 1939. It was about 7 feet tall and could speak around 700 words.

ICT (Information and Communication Technology) is very useful, but it also has some problems.

Good points (Advantages):

  • We can share information very fast.
  • Talking to people is cheaper using apps like WhatsApp, Facebook, or Skype.
  • People can buy things online anytime.
  • Students can study from home using the internet.
  • ICT has created new jobs like computer programmers, web designers, and engineers.

Bad points (Disadvantages):

  • People can become addicted to phones and spend less time with others.
  • Children may waste too much time playing games.
  • Some people lose their jobs because computers do the work instead of humans.
  • False information can spread easily.

The main components are Hardware and Software.

1. Hardware:

Hardware is the physical parts of a computer that we can touch, like keyboard, mouse, and monitor. Hardware can be divided into:

  • a. Input Devices
  • b. Output Devices
  • c. Communication Devices
  • d. System Unit
  • e. Storage Devices

2. Software:

The programs inside a computer are called Software. They are divided into two main types based on the job they do for the computer and the user.

  • a. Application Software
  • b. System Software

Application Software: The software that are used to solve the problems of the user are called Application Software e.g MS Word, Paint etc

System Software: The software that are used to solve the problems of the System (Computer) are called System Software e.g Windows, Antivirus etc.

Input Devices: Input devices are used to enter data and instructions into the computer. Example: Keyboard, mouse, microphone, scanner etc

Output Devices: Output devices are used to show or give the results after the computer processes the data. Example: Printer, display screen, speaker, multimedia projector.

Here are some input devices:

  • Keyboard: This is one of the most important input devices. We use it to type letters, numbers, characters, and give commands to the computer.
  • Mouse: A mouse helps us move the cursor on the screen and click buttons to select things or give instructions.
  • Microphone: It lets us speak into the computer. The computer can record our voice or understand what we say.
  • Digital Camera: We use a digital camera to take photos or videos. These pictures and videos can then be stored and used in the computer.
  • Biometric Device: This device checks a person’s identity using their fingerprint, face, eyes (iris), or voice. It is often used for security, like unlocking phones or entering rooms.
  • Hand Held Scanner: You hold this scanner in your hand and move it over an object (like a barcode) to read the code and put the information into the computer.
  • Flat Bed Scanner: It has a flat glass surface. You place a book, photo, or paper on it to scan and copy the image into the computer.
  • QR Code Reader: This device reads QR codes. It quickly gets the information stored inside the QR code and sends it to the computer.
  • Digital Sensor: Sensors detect changes around them, such as motion, light, heat, smoke, or sound. They send this information to the computer. For example, a motion sensor can turn on lights when someone moves.

Here are some output devices:

  • Display Screen: This is also called a VDU (Visual Display Unit). It shows pictures and text on a screen. There are different types, like LCD, LED, and SMD.
  • Printer: This gives us a hard copy of our work. It puts our documents or photos onto paper. Common types include Inkjet, Laser, and even 3D printers.
  • Speaker: This allows us to hear sound from the computer, such as music, videos, or game noises.
  • Multimedia Projector: This is used to show a very large version of the computer screen on a wall. It is great for showing lessons to a whole class at once.

Hard Copy: Hard copy is a copy of textual or graphic information printed on paper.

Soft Copy: Softcopy is information displayed on screen or stored in computer.

Communication devices are devices that help computers and other devices to share information with each other over networks or the internet.

Here are some communication devices:

  • Network Switch: A network switch is a device that connects many devices in a network. It sends messages from one device to the correct device in the network.
  • Router: A router is a device that sends data from one network to another. It also finds the best way for the data to travel.
  • Wireless Access Point (WAP): A WAP is a device that lets computers and phones connect to the network without wires. It works with a wired router.
  • Bluetooth: Bluetooth is a short-range wireless technology. It helps devices like phones, computers, headphones, and speakers connect without wires.
  • DSL modem: A DSL modem is a device that connects to the internet using regular telephone lines. It gives fast internet to your computer.

The System Unit is the main case or "box" that protects all the electronic parts of a computer. It holds the most important components that help the computer think and work.

Here are the most important parts inside:

  • Motherboard: This is the main circuit board. It acts like a "glue" that holds all the parts (CPU, RAM, and connectors) together so they can talk to each other.
  • CPU (Central Processing Unit): This is the brain of the computer. It follows instructions and does all the math and logic to make things happen.
  • RAM (Random Access Memory): This is temporary memory. It stores data that the CPU is using right now. Having more RAM makes the computer faster!
  • ROM (Read Only Memory): This is permanent memory. It stays on even when the computer is turned off. It contains the instructions that tell the computer how to start up.
  • Power Supply: This part takes electricity from the wall and changes it into the right kind of power for the computer parts.
  • Ports: These are the holes on the outside (like USB ports or Speaker ports) where you plug in your mouse, keyboard, or headphones.

Data: Data is raw facts or pieces of information that are not yet organized. It can be numbers, words, or symbols that need to be processed to make sense.

Example: The names and marks of students are data before the computer makes a report card.

Information: Information is data that has been organized or processed so that it is useful and easy to understand.

Example: When the computer makes a report card from students’ marks, that report card is information.

The Process Flow:

The computer follows a simple path to turn facts into results: Input (Data) → Process → Output (Information)

Storage Devices are special tools used to keep our data safe forever. Unlike a computer’s temporary memory, these devices do not forget your files even when you turn the power off.

Here are some storage devices:

  • Hard Disk (Internal or External): This is the main storage "closet" for the computer. It can hold a huge amount of data, like all your programs, games, and photos.
  • Flash Drive (USB): This is a small, portable device. People use it to carry their files in their pockets and move them from one computer to another.
  • Optical Storage (CD, DVD, Blu-ray): These are shiny discs that use laser light to read and write information. They are often used for movies, music, or backup files.
  • Memory Card: These are very small, flat cards. We use them in portable gadgets like digital cameras, smartphones, and tablets to save pictures and videos.
