Problem: A problem is a challenge or a situation that we need to fix. We deal with different problems in our life every day.

Problem Solving: Problem Solving means looking at a situation carefully. We think about it and then find the right way to solve it.

There are two kinds of problems:

  • i. Simple Problems: These are easy problems. They usually have only one answer.
  • ii. Complex Problems: These are hard problems. They can be tricky. You might need to break a big problem into small parts to solve it. These problems can have many answers.

Example: How will you prepare for examination to achieve good marks?

You can solve the problem by following these steps:

  1. Follow a daily schedule for your study.
  2. Read your syllabus and look at your books and notes.
  3. Work on the exercise questions in your book.
  4. Practice using sample papers (old tests).
  5. Talk to your friends if you do not understand a topic.

To solve any problem, we follow 6 steps. These steps help us to find the best answer.

  1. Step 1: Identify a Problem: First, find out what is wrong. Write 1 or 2 short sentences to explain the problem clearly.
  2. Step 2: Decompose a Problem: A big problem can be complex. Break it into small parts. These small parts are called sub-problems.
  3. Step 3: Generate Potential Solutions: Think of many ways to fix the problem. Look for all the possible answers you can find.
  4. Step 4: Select and Design the Ideal Solution: Compare your answers. Pick the best one. The best choice is the one that is easy to do and fits your needs.
  5. Step 5: Implementing the Solution: This is the action step. Start doing the best plan you picked.
  6. Step 6: Evaluate the Solution: Check your work. Ask, "Did it work?" If not, you can change the plan to make it better.

Example 1: How can Hamza make healthy French fries?

Situation: Hamza loves fries, but he is a bit heavy. His mother wants him to be healthy.

  1. Problem Identification: How to make healthy fries with less fat?
  2. Problem Decomposition: What do we need? What is the recipe? How to cook them?
  3. Potential Solutions: Use a pan, Use an air-fryer, Use a deep-fryer.
  4. Select an Ideal Solution: The air-fryer is the best. It uses very little oil and has fewer calories.
  5. Implementing the Solution: Cook the fries in the air-fryer.
  6. Evaluate the Solution: If the fries are not soft, cook them for a few more minutes. Eat them with a salad to stay healthy.

Algorithm: An algorithm is a list of steps to solve a problem in a clear and correct way.

We must remember:

  • We have limited time ⏰
  • We have limited resources 📦

So, we should solve problems in a smart way.

Algorithmic Thinking: Algorithmic thinking is the special skill of creating these steps in the right order. It is a very important part of problem-solving. This skill helps you solve Computer programming problems, Mathematics problems, and Real-life challenges.

There are six properties of an algorithm:

  1. Input: This is the data or info we give to the computer to start.
  2. Output: This is the final result or the answer we get at the end.
  3. Definiteness: Every step must be clear. There should be no confusion.
  4. Effectiveness: Every step must be possible. You must be able to do the work.
  5. Finiteness: The steps must stop. An algorithm cannot go on forever.
  6. Generality: The steps should work for many similar problems.

We write algorithms in natural language. This just means using plain English so that everyone can read and understand the steps easily.

Using algorithms to solve problems has many benefits:

  • a. Easy to Understand: It uses a step-by-step way to show the solution.
  • b. Definite Procedure: It follows a fixed and clear set of rules.
  • c. Independent: It does not depend on any specific computer language. You can write it in plain English.
  • d. Logical Sequence: Each step follows a logical order. This makes it very easy to find and fix mistakes (errors).

A person can follow these steps:

  1. Take a spanner and loosen the nuts
  2. Place the jack in the correct position
  3. Lift the car
  4. Remove the nuts and take off the wheel
  5. Change the wheel
  6. Lower the car
  7. Tighten the nuts properly

Solution constructs are used when steps do not always follow the same order or we need to change steps.

There are two main types:

  • Conditional Statement
  • Repetition

It is a way to make a decision using IF and THEN.

  • IF means the "Question" or "Condition."
  • THEN means the "Result" or "Action."

Example: Days of the Week

IF today is Monday, THEN yesterday was Sunday.

Repetition means doing the same task again and again. It must have a point where it stops.

  3. If allowed, issue the book

Potential Solution: Any possible way to solve a problem. It may not be the best way.

Characteristics: Workable but might be slow, expensive, or difficult.

Example: Using a knife to sharpen a broken pencil.

Ideal Solution: The best possible way to solve a problem. Most efficient, safe, and easiest.

Example: Using a pencil sharpener.

IF the laptop battery is less than 10%, THEN plug in the charger. Else, keep working.
